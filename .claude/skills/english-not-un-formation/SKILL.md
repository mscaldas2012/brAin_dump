# English Not Un- Formation

Use this skill when prose hedges through double negatives — *not unimportant, not unreasonable, not unlike, not without merit* — instead of asserting the positive directly.

Source concept: The not un- formation is a verbal habit that can spread through prose like a weed. It is always possible to cure oneself of it by memorizing: "A not unblack dog was chasing a not unsmall rabbit across a not ungreen field." The cure is to replace every not un- construction with the direct positive — or, where the middle state is genuinely intended, to name it plainly. (Orwell, "Politics and the English Language," 1946)

## Rules

- **Replace *not un-* with the direct positive**: *not unimportant* → *important*; *not unreasonable* → *reasonable*; *not unlikely* → *likely* or *probable*; *not uncommon* → *common* or *frequent*; *not uninteresting* → *interesting.* The double negative saves no words, adds cognitive friction, and often signals reluctance to commit.
- **Replace *not in-* and similar formations**: *not incapable* → *capable*; *not inconsiderable* → *considerable* (or name the quantity); *not insignificant* → *significant*; *not impossible* → *possible* or *likely.* These are the same habit with a different prefix.
- **Replace oblique softeners that work the same way**: *less than helpful* → *unhelpful*; *more than adequate* → *sufficient* or *good*; *not without merit* → *meritorious* or name the merit; *not entirely accurate* → *inaccurate* or *partly wrong.* These constructions hedge by denying the negative of the positive rather than asserting the negative directly.
- **The genuine middle state**: *not unhappy* is not the same as *happy* — there is a logical interval between them. When the middle state is genuinely what you mean, name it directly: *content but not elated; acceptable but not impressive; tolerable rather than good.* Do not use the double negative as a way of avoiding commitment; use it only when the middle state is the precise claim, and even then prefer a positive name for that state.
- The formation is often a sign of **hedging under pressure** — the writer wants to imply something without quite saying it. The cure is to ask: what exactly is the claim? Then say that.

## Objective Rubric

- No *not un-* construction remains where the direct positive (or direct negative) conveys the same claim.
- No *not in-* or equivalent prefix construction remains where the affirmative form is available.
- No oblique softener (*less than helpful, not without merit, more than adequate*) remains where a direct positive or negative word serves.
- Where a double negative was retained for a genuine middle state, the revision names that state explicitly and positively.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
