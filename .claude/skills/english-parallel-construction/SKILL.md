# English Parallel Construction

Use this skill when conceptually coordinate ideas are not expressed in structurally parallel form — when a series, list, or paired construction drifts across grammatical categories, depriving the reader of the structural signal that the ideas are equal.

Source concept: "The principle of parallel construction requires that expressions similar in content and function be outwardly similar. The likeness of form enables the reader to recognise more readily the likeness of content and function." (Strunk, *The Elements of Style*, 1918)

## Rules

- **The coordinate-ideas test**: before applying this skill, confirm that the ideas actually are coordinate — genuinely equal in type, function, and logical status. If they are, they must take parallel grammatical form. If they are not (one is a main point, another is a qualification), use subordination rather than false coordination.
- **Series and lists**: every item in a comma-separated series must be the same grammatical form — all nouns, all infinitives, all gerunds, all clauses. *"He liked reading, writing, and to walk"* → *"He liked reading, writing, and walking."* The form of the first item sets the pattern; all subsequent items must follow it.
- **Correlative constructions**: *both...and*, *either...or*, *neither...nor*, *not only...but also*, *as...as*, *more...than* — both sides of a correlative pair must be the same grammatical form. The test: whatever grammatical structure follows the first correlative must be mirrored after the second. *"He was not only brave but also showed wisdom"* → *"He was not only brave but also wise."* (adjective paired with adjective); or *"He not only fought bravely but also chose wisely."* (verb phrase paired with verb phrase).
- **Achieved parallelism as a rhetorical device**: parallel construction is not merely a grammatical requirement — it is the chief tool of balanced prose and rhythmic emphasis. Caesar's *veni, vidi, vici*; Lincoln's *of the people, by the people, for the people*. When ideas are genuinely equal, making their grammatical structure identical makes their equality audible. Reach for parallel form deliberately when building a climactic list or a balanced opposition.
- Distinguish from `english-unequal-yokefellows`: unequal-yokefellows diagnoses mismatched elements that were wrongly joined as if parallel. This skill addresses the positive obligation — when ideas are genuinely parallel, make the structure match.

## Objective Rubric

- Every series or list in the revision uses the same grammatical form for all items (all nouns, all infinitives, all gerunds, all clauses).
- Every correlative pair (*both...and*, *either...or*, *not only...but also*) is balanced: the grammatical structure following the first correlative is mirrored after the second.
- No item in a series requires a different reading of the sentence's grammar than the item that precedes it.
- Where deliberate rhetorical parallelism is present, the parallel structure is exact — not approximate.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
