# English Concrete Language

Use this skill when prose is vague, abstract, or general where it could be specific, definite, or concrete — when the reader is told that something happened rather than shown what happened.

Source concept: "Prefer the specific to the general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract. If your experiences would lead you to write 'A period of unfavorable weather set in,' say instead 'It rained every day for a week.'" (Strunk, *The Elements of Style*, 1918; see also Fowler & Fowler, *The King's English*, 1908, Vocabulary Rule 2)

## Rules

- **The abstraction ladder**: most concepts can be stated at different levels of specificity. *Livestock → cattle → cow → Bessie.* Move down the ladder: name the specific animal, object, person, number, or action rather than the category it belongs to. The general claim can be made in the topic sentence; the body of the paragraph should descend to the specific.
- **Replace vague quantities with numbers or proportions**: *"a large number of"* → *"most"* or *"forty-seven"*; *"a period of time"* → *"three weeks"*; *"many scholars"* → *"a dozen scholars"* or name them. If the number is genuinely unknown, say *"several"* or *"a few"* — but do not use vague quantifiers as a substitute for research.
- **Replace abstract descriptions with specific actions or properties**: *"an interesting result"* → name what made it interesting; *"a significant improvement"* → name the amount of improvement; *"he showed courage"* → name the specific act of courage; *"conditions were difficult"* → name one condition.
- **Anchor abstractions with examples**: when a general claim or abstract concept is necessary — and it sometimes is — follow it immediately with a concrete instance. The pattern is *general claim → specific example → return to general (optional)*. Do not let a paragraph remain at the abstract level throughout.
- Abstract nouns that encode actions should be converted to verbs (see `english-noun-verb-inversion`). The concrete-language principle extends further: even when the verb is restored, the noun choices throughout a sentence should prefer the specific and nameable over the categorical and vague.

## Objective Rubric

- No vague quantity (*a large number of, many, several instances of, a period of*) remains where a specific number, proportion, or time frame is available or could be researched.
- Every abstract description that reports a quality (*interesting, significant, difficult, important*) is followed by a specific example or property that gives the quality content.
- Every general category (*an animal, a tool, a location, a person*) is replaced by a specific name or type where the specifics are known and relevant.
- Where abstraction is retained, it is anchored within the same paragraph by at least one concrete example.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
