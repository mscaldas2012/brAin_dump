# English Elegant Variation

Use this skill when reviewing prose where synonyms, circumlocutions, or pronoun substitutions have been introduced to avoid repeating a word.

Source concept: Elegant variation is the writer's anxiety to avoid repetition leading to substitutions that are absurd or misleading — misguided because repeating the right word is a virtue, not a fault, and a synonym invented to avoid it introduces ambiguity about whether the referent has changed. (Fowler & Fowler, *The King's English*, 1908)

## Rules

- When a word must recur, prefer repeating the same word — or using a pronoun (*it*, *its*, *they*, *their*) where the antecedent is immediately clear — over introducing a synonym to escape the repetition.
- Test every synonym substitution: does the new term carry a different shade of meaning that the context requires? If not, restore the original word.
- Elegant variation is most damaging in expository, argumentative, and non-fiction prose, where the reader uses consistent terminology to track a concept or entity across sentences. A shifted term signals — falsely — that the subject has changed.
- Circumlocutions used as variation ("the committee" → "the body charged with oversight") are doubly harmful: they pad the prose and amplify the ambiguity.
- Deliberate variation is legitimate only when the new term introduces a genuine contrast, irony, or meaning distinction the context requires. The test is intentionality and semantic difference.

## Objective Rubric

- Every synonym substitution in the revision either carries a distinct meaning the context requires, or the original word is restored.
- The reader can track the subject, concept, or entity through the passage using consistent terminology.
- No word has been replaced by a circumlocution purely to avoid repetition.
- Where a varied term has been restored to the original, no new ambiguity about the referent has been introduced.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
