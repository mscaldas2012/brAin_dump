# English Dying Metaphors

Use this skill when reviewing prose for stale figurative language, metaphors that produce no mental image, or incompatible figures mixed in the same clause.

Source concept: A dying metaphor occupies the hazardous middle zone between a fresh metaphor, which evokes a visual image, and a dead metaphor, which has become an ordinary word — it has lost its evocative power but still carries the pretence of a figure, and is used because it saves the writer the trouble of inventing fresh language. (Orwell, "Politics and the English Language," 1946)

## Rules

- Distinguish three zones: **fresh** (calls up a specific image — earn it), **dead** (fully absorbed into ordinary language, e.g. *iron resolution* — no residual figurative force, leave it), **dying** (worn-out, still pretending to be figurative — eliminate or replace).
- Test every metaphor: does it call up a specific, coherent visual or sensory image? If the answer is vague or automatic, the metaphor is dying.
- Treat mixed metaphors as a diagnostic sign: incompatible figures in the same clause prove the writer has stopped seeing images and is assembling verbal parts by habit. Mixed metaphors must always be repaired.
- Replace a dying metaphor either with a fresh figure that earns its image, or with plain literal statement. Do not replace one cliché with another.
- When a sentence stacks multiple dying metaphors, prefer plain literal statement over any fresh figure — no single image can carry the meanings of four stacked clichés without grotesque overload.
- Never use a figure of speech you have seen many times in print without asking what it actually depicts.

## Objective Rubric

- Every metaphor in the revision either (a) calls up a specific, coherent image the reader can visualize, or (b) is a fully dead idiom with no residual figurative pretension.
- No two figures in the same clause are visually incompatible.
- No dying metaphor has been replaced by a different dying metaphor.
- Where a dying metaphor was removed, the full literal meaning is preserved in the revision.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
