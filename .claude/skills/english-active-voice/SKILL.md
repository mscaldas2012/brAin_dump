# English Active Voice

Use this skill when prose is weakened by passive constructions that could be active, or by expletive constructions (*there is, it was, it can be seen that*) that delay the real subject. Distinct from `english-stationary-passive`, which diagnoses the specific pattern of auxiliary chains with no expressed agent; this skill addresses the broader preference for active voice and its principled exceptions.

Source concept: "The active voice is usually more direct and vigorous than the passive. Many a tame sentence of description or exposition can be made lively and emphatic by substituting a transitive in the active voice." (Strunk, *The Elements of Style*, 1918)

## Rules

- Active voice places the actor as the grammatical subject of a transitive verb: the sentence moves from agent to action to object. This is the most direct path. *"The committee approved the proposal"* not *"The proposal was approved by the committee."*
- **Expletive constructions** (*there is, there are, there was, it is, it was, it can be seen that, it should be noted that*) delay the real subject and real verb. Replace them: *"There was a man who survived"* → *"A man survived"*; *"It was noticed that the door was open"* → name who noticed: *"She noticed the door was open."*
- The passive is **legitimate** in these situations: (a) when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant (*"Three people were injured"*); (b) when the object of the action is more important as grammatical subject than the actor (*"The president was assassinated"*); (c) when the actor has already been established and the sentence's focus is the action's result (*"He proposed; the motion was seconded and carried"*); (d) in scientific and procedural writing where the object of the study, not the researcher, is the subject.
- **The by-phrase test**: if a passive construction has an expressed agent in a *by-* phrase (*"was approved by the committee"*), the active construction is almost always better. The existence of the *by-* phrase proves the agent is known and important — make them the grammatical subject.
- Distinguish from `english-stationary-passive`: the stationary passive is a specific diagnostic pattern (auxiliary chains with no expressed agent). This skill addresses all passive constructions — including those with expressed agents — in relation to the principle of directness and vigor.

## Objective Rubric

- Every passive construction with an expressed *by-* phrase is converted to active voice.
- No expletive construction (*there is, there are, it was, it can be seen that*) remains where the real subject and verb can be named.
- Every retained passive is explicitly categorized as legitimate: unknown agent / object-emphasis / established agent / scientific convention.
- No meaning has been lost or distorted in the conversion from passive to active.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
