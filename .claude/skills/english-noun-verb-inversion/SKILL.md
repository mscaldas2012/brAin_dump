# English Noun-Verb Inversion

Use this skill when the controlling problem in a passage is nominalization — verbs converted into abstract nouns supported by weak carrying verbs.

Source concept: Noun-verb inversion converts an action expressed as a transitive verb (*won or lost*) into an abstract nominal construction (*had losses or gains*), draining the sentence of energy by replacing the word that names what happens with a word that names a category of event, carried by a colourless support verb. (Quiller-Couch, *On the Art of Writing*, Lecture V, 1916)

## Rules

- The pattern: **action verb** → **abstract noun** (ending in *-tion, -sion, -ment, -ance, -ence, -al, -ure*) + **weak support verb** (*make, give, take, have, perform, carry out, effect, achieve, reach, conduct, provide*). Restore the action verb: *"made a decision"* → *"decided"*; *"conducted an investigation"* → *"investigated"*; *"reached an agreement"* → *"agreed"*; *"gave consideration to"* → *"considered"*.
- The **-tion** nominalization is the most common form: *clarification, determination, utilization, implementation, finalization, optimization.* The verb form is almost always shorter and stronger.
- The **support-verb test**: if a sentence contains *make, give, take, have, perform, carry out, conduct, effect, achieve, provide* followed by a noun derived from a verb, inversion is almost certainly present.
- Inversion is not always wrong — a nominalization is legitimate when the abstract noun is the **actual topic** of the sentence (*the investigation took three months* = the investigation as a thing, not the act of investigating, is what took time), or when the noun form has no strong single-verb equivalent in context.
- Some nominalizations use suffixes not in the standard list (*defense* from *defend*, *response* from *respond*, *approval* from *approve*) or no suffix at all (*attack, research, attempt, request*). The support-verb test may not catch these, but the general principle applies: if an abstract noun could be replaced by its base verb with the noun's implied agent as subject — *"has attracted serious scholarly defense from theologians"* → *"theologians have defended it seriously"* — prefer the verb form.
- Do not conflate with the stationary passive: *"an investigation was conducted"* is both inverted and passive; *"the committee conducted an investigation"* is inverted but active. This skill addresses the inversion regardless of voice.

## Objective Rubric

- Every *-tion/-sion/-ment/-ance/-ence* nominalization in the revision is either (a) the genuine topic of the sentence with no single-verb equivalent, or (b) converted to its verb form.
- No weak support verb (*make, give, take, have, perform, carry out, conduct, effect, achieve*) carries an abstract noun where a single action verb would serve.
- No verb-derived noun with a non-standard suffix (*defense, response, approval, attempt, request*) functions as the grammatical object of any verb where restoring the base verb with its implied agent would produce a shorter, clearer sentence.
- The revision is shorter than the original with no loss of meaning.
- No restored verb has altered or narrowed the meaning of the sentence.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
