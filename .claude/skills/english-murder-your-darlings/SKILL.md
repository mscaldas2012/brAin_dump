# English Murder Your Darlings

Use this skill when reviewing prose for passages that exist primarily because the writer finds them pleasing — extended metaphors that overshadow the argument, digressions that showcase expertise, or highly wrought sentences that do no more work than a plain version would.

Source concept: "Whenever you feel an impulse to perpetrate a piece of exceptionally fine writing, obey it — whole-heartedly — and delete it before sending your manuscript to press. Murder your darlings." A darling is any passage you are excessively fond of; that fondness is the diagnostic sign that it is answering your satisfaction, not the reader's need. (Quiller-Couch, *On the Art of Writing*, 1916)

## Rules

- **The function test**: for any suspect passage, ask what the reader would lose if it were removed. If the argument, narrative, or explanation would be weaker or incomplete, keep it. If nothing would be lost except the pleasure of the writing itself, it is a darling — cut it.
- **Signs of a darling**: (a) an analogy or extended metaphor that runs longer than the point it illustrates; (b) a digression into a topic that exists to display the writer's knowledge or taste rather than to serve the reader's understanding; (c) a sentence or paragraph the writer clearly labored over but that does no more work than a plain version would; (d) a passage that repeats a point already made more strongly elsewhere, kept because the writer prefers this version's phrasing.
- **The compression test**: can the suspect passage be compressed to half its length without losing anything the reader needs? If yes, the uncut version is a darling. If compression loses essential content, the passage is earning its length.
- **The stranger test**: would a competent editor with no emotional investment in this prose include this passage? If the honest answer is probably not, the emotional investment is the tell — cut it.
- Distinguish from genuinely fine writing that earns its place: a passage is not a darling because it is well-written or pleasurable to read. It becomes a darling when the writer's fondness for it is the primary reason it exists, rather than the work it does. The diagnosis is motivational, not aesthetic.

## Objective Rubric

- The removed or compressed section did not carry information, argument, or narrative not conveyed more effectively elsewhere.
- The shortened version makes the argument or explanation at least as clear to a reader approaching it fresh.
- No remaining passage is longer than its function requires: every sentence is earning its presence.
- The function test confirms that nothing the reader needed was removed — the cut was of writer-satisfaction, not reader-value.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
