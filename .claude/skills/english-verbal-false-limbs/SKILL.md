# English Verbal False Limbs

Use this skill when prose is padded with verb-phrases where single verbs would serve, or when sentences end in vague resounding qualifications.

Source concept: Verbal false limbs save the trouble of picking out appropriate verbs and nouns and at the same time pad each sentence with extra syllables that give it an appearance of symmetry — replacing a single precise verb with a phrase built from a noun or adjective tacked onto a general-purpose verb such as *prove, serve, form, play, render.* (Orwell, "Politics and the English Language," 1946)

## Rules

- Replace inflated verb-phrases with single precise verbs: *render inoperative* → *break*; *give rise to* → *cause*; *make contact with* → *reach*; *have the effect of reducing* → *reduce*; *serve the purpose of* → cut the phrase entirely and restructure. If a sentence contains *prove, serve, form, play, render, exhibit, or make* followed by a noun or adjective, suspect a false limb.
- Restore simple prepositions and conjunctions: *with respect to* → *on* or *about*; *having regard to* → *given*; *in view of the fact that* → *because*; *in the interests of* → *for*; *with a view to* → *to*. Compound prepositions almost always exist to avoid committing to a direct relationship.
- Eliminate *-ize* and *de-* formations invented to avoid committing to a specific meaning: *finalize* → *finish* or *settle*; *operationalize* → *put into practice*; *prioritize* (in most uses) → *rank* or name what comes first. Test: does the *-ize* word do anything a plain verb cannot? If not, cut it.
- Remove resounding tail-piece commonplaces that give sentences a cushioned landing in place of a specific claim: *greatly to be desired, cannot be left out of account, deserving of serious consideration, a development to be expected in the near future, brought to a satisfactory conclusion.* Cut the tail or replace it with the specific claim it evades.
- Prefer gerunds over noun constructions: *by examining* over *by means of examination of*; *after deciding* over *following the making of a decision regarding.*

## Objective Rubric

- No *prove, serve, form, play, render, exhibit, make* carries a noun or adjective where a single precise verb would serve.
- No compound preposition (*with respect to, in view of, in the interests of, having regard to, with a view to*) appears where a single preposition or conjunction would express the relation directly.
- No *-ize* or *de-* formation appears that avoids a more specific plain verb.
- No sentence ends in a vague commonplace (*greatly to be desired, deserving of serious consideration*) in place of a specific claim.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
