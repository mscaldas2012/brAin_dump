Chapter Three

Elena had long dark hair and piercing blue eyes that seemed to see right through people. She stood at the window, watching the rain fall against the glass. It was a dark and stormy evening, and the weather seemed to mirror the turmoil she felt inside.

"I don't understand why you did it," she said softly, turning to face Marcus.

Marcus crossed his arms. He felt a deep wave of anger wash over him. "Because I had no choice," he replied defensively.

She looked away. He uncrossed his arms. She crossed to the table. He watched her.

"There's always a choice," she retorted sharply. "As you know, Marcus, the protocol requires explicit authorization before any transfer of funds. You were aware of that when you signed the agreement."

"Elena—"

"Don't." She held up her hand firmly.

Suddenly, the door burst open. It was Director Calloway, and the expression on his face told Elena everything she needed to know. Her heart raced. A shiver ran down her spine. She couldn't help but notice how his eyes met hers across the room before sliding to Marcus with unmistakable fury.

"Both of you," Calloway intoned gravely, "in my office. Now."

Elena wondered if she had made the right choice in trusting Marcus. She thought about everything that had led to this moment — the late nights, the whispered conversations, the promises. Perhaps loyalty was more complicated than she had once believed. Perhaps trust itself was a kind of gamble, a tapestry of hope woven against the dark uncertainty of human nature.

Marcus exhaled deeply. He nodded reluctantly. He reached for his jacket. He straightened his collar. "Let's go," he murmured quietly.

The walk down the hallway felt like an eternity. Every step seemed to carry the weight of what they had done, the consequences that were now, undeniably, beginning to unfold.
