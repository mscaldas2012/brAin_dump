# English Stationary Passive

Use this skill when the controlling problem in a passage is passive voice — specifically when agency is buried in auxiliary-participle chains rather than expressed through an active transitive verb.

Source concept: The stationary passive is a passive construction built on chains of auxiliary verbs (*was, is, being, been, have been, will be*) and participles that remove the acting agent from the sentence — called "stationary" because nothing moves; there is no agent striking an object. Use transitive verbs that strike their object; eschew the stationary passive. (Quiller-Couch, *On the Art of Writing*, Lecture V, 1916)

## Rules

- The **stationary passive** is an auxiliary-participle chain with no expressed or recoverable agent: *was brought to the attention of, has been given consideration, is being reviewed by the relevant parties, will have been resolved.* It is called stationary because the sentence describes a state, not an action with a doer.
- **Legitimate passive** exists and must not be eliminated: use the passive (a) when the agent is genuinely unknown or irrelevant (*The bridge was built in 1887*), (b) when placing the recipient as grammatical subject is the deliberate point (*Keats was neglected in his lifetime*), or (c) in scientific and procedural writing where the acting agent is understood by convention.
- To diagnose: ask who is performing the action. If the answer is "someone" and that agent can be named or recovered from context, restructure with the agent as subject of an active verb.
- Replace stationary passive constructions with the transitive form: *"He was made the recipient of a silver teapot"* → *"They gave him a silver teapot."* The agent (*they*) takes the subject position; the verb (*gave*) strikes its object.
- Auxiliary chains longer than two words (*was being considered, will have been reviewed, had been brought to the attention of*) are almost always stationary passives; treat them as a warning signal.

## Objective Rubric

- Every passive construction in the revision is either (a) a legitimate passive with a stated reason, or (b) converted to an active transitive construction with a recoverable agent as subject.
- No auxiliary chain longer than two words remains where an agent can be identified.
- The agent of each action is either the grammatical subject of an active verb, or is genuinely unknown/irrelevant and the passive is justified.
- No meaning has been lost or distorted in the conversion to active voice.

Pass only when every applicable check passes.
